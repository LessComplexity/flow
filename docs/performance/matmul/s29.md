# S29 — the KC k-panel verdict, and the first honest kernel-only shape numbers

Machine: **M4 Pro (local), clean idle, min-of-3**. Legs are compute-only.
Baselines: clang++ `-O3 -march=native -ffp-contract=fast`, rustc `-O -C
target-cpu=native`, NumPy on **Accelerate/AMX** (local — the box's OpenBLAS
frontier is a separate, not-yet-run leg). Sizes per the standing rule: fir 1M,
conv2d 1024, matmul 1024.

Two things changed how these numbers are produced, and both matter more than any
single row below:

1. **The Flow shapes now time themselves** (`() -> time`, plan-time-builtin).
   `FLOW_PERF` is retired for `benches/shapes/` — it bracketed all of
   `flow_main`, so every Flow leg carried its data generation while every
   baseline timed the kernel alone (the S28 gen-boundary finding).
2. **A clock read fences the work written above it.** Without that, the bracket
   measures nothing under the parallel orchestrator; see §3.

## 1. The KC k-panel nest: a 3× LOSS, gated off

`benches/matmul/tile_ab.sh matmul1024_cap_f32`, FLOW_PAR=1, min-of-3, FLOW_PERF
total ms (the matmul harness is not migrated to `time` — recorded, §5):

| leg | KC nest ON | KC nest OFF | S28 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| tile | 59.80 | 33.75 | — |
| **fma** | **59.82** | **19.80** | **18.9** |
| no-pack | 40.79 | 36.25 | — |
| no-tile | 890.27 | 839.22 | — |

The OFF column reproduces S28 (19.8 vs 18.9), so the rest of the tree is intact
and the regression is the nest itself: **3.0× slower at 1024 f32**.

**Why — diagnosed, not assumed (the first explanation was wrong).** The session first
attributed this to parking traffic: the (jc, kc, ic) order runs other i-blocks between
two k-panels of the same block, so partial sums cannot stay in scratch and must be
parked in `out` once per panel. That story is **refuted by its own control**. Parking
traffic scales with the panel count, so a `TILE_KC` sweep varies it 4×:

| `TILE_KC` | panels (K/KC) | parking traffic | ms (min-of-3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 128 | 8 | 62.9 MB | 56.37 |
| 256 | 4 | 29.4 MB | 55.40 |
| 512 | 2 | 12.6 MB | 55.09 |

Removing 80% of the parking buys 1.3%. Extrapolated, **all** parking costs ~0.9 ms of a
35 ms gap.

The real cause is in the machine code. Both legs emit the **identical** micro-kernel IR
— same 8 FMAs, same 2 B-loads, same GEPs — and execute it the identical 8,388,608 times.
But `clang` promotes the `[64 x float]` accumulator alloca into NEON registers for the
whole k range in the OFF leg and **fails to in the ON leg**:

| | OFF | ON |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `str q…, [sp]` in the tiled task | **0** | **92** |
| `ldr q…, [sp]` | 0 | 56 |
| runtime alias checks (`ccmp`) | 0 | 8 |
| scalar `fmadd` (dead fallback) | 2 | 32 |
| micro-kernel instructions / 2 k-steps | 51 | 109 |

OFF's inner loop holds all 16 accumulator vectors in `v0-v7`/`v16-v23` and touches memory
zero times; ON does `ldr q25,[sp,#0xb10]` → `fmla.4s` → `str q25,[sp,#0xb10]` for every
accumulator on every k-step. That is 2.14× the instructions and ~5.9 GB of extra L1
traffic — about 100× the parking traffic it was blamed on — with a store-to-load
forwarding dependence now sitting on each accumulator's recurrence. OFF runs at the FMA
issue limit (~4 cycles per k-step); ON at ~10.6.

It is **not** register pressure (ON touches *fewer* distinct vector registers, 25 vs 27,
and leaves `v10`-`v15` idle), **not** a vectorization loss (16 `fmla.4s` per k-step in
both, no narrowing), and **not** the tile constants — every `TILE_KC` ∈ {128, 256, 512}
spills identically, and so does every `NC`. A second sweep over the j-block width shows
the same: collapsing to one jb block (`NC` = 1024) recovers 56.4 → 39.1 ms by removing
j-tile preheaders and parks, but the inner loop still spills and it stays 1.8× off the
baseline.

**Not yet pinned:** the exact reason promotion fails. A hand-applied `!noalias` on the
panel pointer did not restore it, and `-Rpass-missed=licm` reports the same class of
remark in both legs (48 vs 32). The alias check versioning ON's loop against a dead
scalar fallback says LLVM could not disambiguate the accumulator scratch from the packed
panel — which the baseline, with the same two pointers, does disambiguate.

**Disposition: kept, tested, `EmitOpts::kc_nest` default OFF.** It is bit-exact
against the untiled emission either way (the differential suite enforces it at
-O0/-O2 across K%KC≠0, C%NC≠0, rows%TI≠0 and FLOW_PAR splits), so it is a pure
performance tailor in ADR-0032's sense.

The diagnosis changes what the open question is. This is **not** a verdict on the
(jc, kc, ic) order — the order's own costs (parking, the A-pack, the extra loop
levels) total ~3% here. It is a verdict on the *emitted form* of the accumulator:
while the k-loop body contains an accumulator load/store pair, any nest shape pays
~2.2×. So the next move is a codegen fix, not a re-tune and not (only) a box run:
make the accumulator tile promotable — SSA values or a fixed-width vector type
instead of an alloca indexed by an induction variable, and enough alias information
that LLVM stops versioning the loop. If that lands, the KC order costs about a
millisecond and the box question becomes worth asking on its merits.

## 2. Shapes, kernel-only (the first honest set)

Scaled sizes, `FIR_N=1048576 CONV_SIDE=1024`:

| shape | flow-conf-par | flow-fma-par | cpp-1t | cpp-mt | rust-1t | rust-mt | numpy-1t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fir 1M | 0.464 | **0.402** | 11.395 | 1.462 | 12.485 | 1.621 | 6.368 |
| conv2d 1024 | 0.462 | 0.445 | 0.256 | **0.133** | 1.793 | 0.316 | 1.635 |

Small sizes, for the scaling read (`FIR_N=65536 CONV_SIDE=512`):

| shape | flow-conf-par | flow-fma-par | cpp-1t | cpp-mt | rust-1t | rust-mt | numpy-1t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| fir 64K | **0.052** | 0.087 | 1.425 | 0.246 | 1.721 | 0.288 | 0.399 |
| conv2d 512 | **0.083** | 0.107 | 0.064 | 0.112 | 0.456 | 0.205 | 0.427 |

**fir wins both tables at both sizes** — 3.6× over cpp-mt and 15.8× over numpy
at 1M, 28× over cpp-1t. The S28 window rung holds at scale.

**conv2d does not scale.** It beats cpp-mt at 512 (0.083 vs 0.112) and loses
3.4× at 1024 (0.445 vs 0.133) — cpp gains superlinearly from 512→1024 while Flow
degrades. That is the recorded TI=1 ceiling (backend-llvm suggestions #11: no
row blocking, so the ×3 image-row reuse across output rows is left on the table)
becoming the dominant term once the image stops fitting the way it did at 512.

**`conf` beats `fma` on the small sizes and on conv2d 1024.** Contraction is not
free here; worth one measurement pass, not a conclusion yet.

### Correction to S28

S28 recorded "conv kernel ≈0.04 ms = 3× over cpp-mt" at 512. That was a
*subtraction* — the FLOW_PERF total minus a separately-timed generation — not a
measurement. The in-source bracket puts the 512 kernel at **0.083 (conf) /
0.107 (fma)**, i.e. still ahead of cpp-mt but by 1.3×, not 3×. The S28 leg-level
claim stands in direction and is wrong in magnitude.

## 3. Why the bracket needed a fence (and why source order is the key)

A `TimeMs` sits on the host spine and consumes only the IO token, so it has **no
value producer to wait for**. Under the parallel orchestrator the host reaches
the second clock read while the tasks it is supposed to be measuring are still
in flight — the first bracketed conv2d run reported `0.000125 ms`, and a second
reported **`-0.000041`**.

Two defects, both fixed in `flow_ir::path_plan`:

- **The fence.** A `TimeMs` checkpoint now waits for the completion of every
  task written entirely before it **in the source**. Source order, because the
  dataflow graph supplies no order at all between pure work and a clock read —
  topo order is free to schedule the whole program on either side of the read,
  and does (a first attempt keyed on topo position produced an empty wait set at
  `t0` and a fence over *everything* at `t1`, i.e. gen+kernel again).
- **The host cone.** `TimeMs` is the first host-spine op that produces a *value*
  rather than only a token. Tasks are dispatched before the host writes it, so a
  task consuming a clock value raced the write — that is the negative elapsed
  (§4.5 Law 1: no transformation reads data not present at its location). The
  whole consumer cone of a clock read now stays on the spine.

Emitted evidence for `conv2d_512.flow`: the wait before `t0` carries 5 entries
(the generation tasks), the one before `t1` carries 7 (generation + kernel).

# S30 addendum — vector-SSA accumulators, and the KC question re-opened

The S29 diagnosis said the KC nest's 3× was a failed register promotion, not traffic, and
that the fix was to stop routing tile accumulators through memory. That landed
(`plan-s30-vector-accumulators.md`): the constant-width main tile now carries `TI`
accumulators of type `<TJ x elem>` as SSA values across the k loop via `phi`, seeded by a
splat and stored to `out` once. No alloca, no GEP, no load/store of accumulator state in
the k loop. Remainder tiles, boundary rows and both TI=1 rungs keep the memory form.

## Before / after (matmul, FLOW_PAR=1, min-of-3, `--contract`)

| size | width | KC | before | after | speedup |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1024 | f32 | off | 20.18 | 20.64 | 0.98× |
| 1024 | f32 | **on** | 59.90 | **21.72** | **2.76×** |
| 1024 | f64 | off | 41.62 | 42.33 | 0.98× |
| 1024 | f64 | **on** | 158.35 | **45.18** | **3.50×** |
| 2048 | f32 | off | 167.88 | 163.53 | 1.03× |
| 2048 | f32 | **on** | 488.54 | **176.30** | **2.77×** |
| 4096 | f32 | off | 1332.20 | 1373.74 | 0.97× |
| 4096 | f32 | **on** | 4096.98 | **1564.14** | **2.62×** |

The shipping path (KC off) is unchanged within noise — it was already being promoted by
LLVM. What changed is that it is now emitted that way rather than granted it.

## The hot loop, verified in the disassembly

| kernel | instrs | fmla | vec loads | vec stores | FMA:mem |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| matmul KC-off | 51 | 32 | 4 | **0** | 8.00 |
| matmul KC-on | 51 | 32 | 4 | **0** | 8.00 |
| fir 1M (window rung) | 62 | 32 | 8 | **0** | 4.00 |
| conv2d 1024 (conv rung) | 93 | 36 | 24 | 4 | 1.29 |

The KC leg's inner loop is now the same shape as the baseline's. Whole-function counts for
the KC task: `str q…,[sp]` **92 → 0**, runtime alias checks (`ccmp`) **8 → 0** — LLVM no
longer versions the loop against a scalar fallback, because there is no longer an address
to disambiguate.

**No accumulator anywhere spills.** fir's window rung and conv2d's conv rung were not
converted (they are TI=1 and were never victims) and both are register-resident already;
their emissions are byte-identical before and after, verified by `cmp`.

## Where KC pays off: nowhere on this machine, and the deficit grows

With both legs optimally emitted, the comparison is finally fair:

| size | KC off | KC on | KC penalty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1024 f32 | 20.64 | 21.72 | +5% |
| 1024 f64 | 42.33 | 45.18 | +7% |
| 2048 f32 | 163.53 | 176.30 | +8% |
| 4096 f32 | 1373.74 | 1564.14 | **+14%** |

The traffic model predicted the opposite: A re-reads scale as C/TJ for the baseline and
C/NC for the nest, so the nest should *gain* with N. It loses more instead. On this
machine the baseline's A re-reads are absorbed by a 16 MB shared L2 and a large SLC, while
the nest's extra work — the A-pack, the park/reload, the deeper nest — is paid in full and
grows with the block count. That is a machine property, not a defect in the order: the
nest was sized for zen3's 512 KB per-core L2, where those re-reads reach DRAM.

**`kc_nest` stays default OFF**, now for a measured reason rather than a broken one. The
box leg is the open question and is finally worth running, because it is now a test of the
*traversal* rather than of a codegen accident.

**Reading it.** Flow beats every naive-class baseline at every size and both widths, and
the margin *grows* with N: 11× → 192× over threaded C++ at f32, 38× → 153× single-thread.
That is the tiling ladder doing its job — the baselines are O(N³) with no blocking, so
they fall off a cliff once the working set leaves cache while Flow does not.

**And numpy is still ahead.** Accelerate/AMX does 4096 f32 in 43.7 ms against Flow's
172.9 — **4.0× ahead**, and 0.1–0.3× across the range, i.e. Flow is 3–9× behind depending
on size. That is the honest frontier: Flow is far past hand-written naive code and not yet
at a tuned BLAS on a machine with a matrix coprocessor. Note this is a *different* gap
from the box's OpenBLAS one (S28 measured 9.7× threaded there) — AMX is dedicated silicon,
so the local number is not the one to chase.

**Consistency check against the old basis.** flow-fma-1t at 1024 f32 is 19.37 ms
kernel-only here against 18.9 ms of FLOW_PERF total in S28 — matmul's generation is small
relative to its kernel, so the two bases nearly coincide at this shape. That is why the
matmul verdicts survived the measurement-boundary bug that broke conv2d's.

**† Two cells are min-of-1.** `rust-1t` and `rust-mt` at 4096 f64 were cut short: at 193

decision — "Flow is ~70× faster" — does not move. The harness should scale its repeat
count to the cell's cost; recorded, not fixed.

## What conv2d's row tells us (suggestion #11, now with a mechanism)

conv2d's hot loop is not accumulator-bound — it is operand-bound: 24 vector loads for 36
FMAs, re-loading all three image rows per output row (`ldr q17,[x3]`, `ldur q18,[x3,#0x4]`,
`ldur q17,[x3,#0x8]`, …) and re-seeding the accumulator inside the loop. That is exactly
the TI=1 ceiling: with row blocking, 6 image rows would serve 4 output rows instead of 3
serving 1. It is the measured explanation for conv2d losing 3.4× at 1024 while winning at
512.
